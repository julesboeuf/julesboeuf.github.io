  Estimating through sampling  

The goal of this project was to use and compare different sampling methods in order to understand their accuracy. These sampling methods were used to try to estimate the population of the Île-de-France region (FRANCE). With this population estimate, we calculated a confidence interval and a margin of error to check how accurate our results were. The work was done using the R statistical software, and the report was written in Word.

The first sampling method used was simple random sampling, meaning every town had an equal chance of being chosen for the sample. The second method was stratified random sampling, where groups (based on population size) were created so the sample would pick evenly from each subgroup; this gives a more representative sample. To compare the two sampling methods, we visualized the results to see which method was more accurate.

On top of the estimates, we also had to reproduce and interpret a Chi-squared independence test, to check whether there was a link between the gender of students and their academic results, from a survey dataset, with the SAS software.

Skills learned:

  • Using the SAS and R software
  • Improving my knowledge and learning the syntax of SAS and R
  • Writing a report
  • Understanding different sampling methods
  • Calculating and interpreting a Chi-squared test to check whether two variables are independent
  • Improving my writing skills
